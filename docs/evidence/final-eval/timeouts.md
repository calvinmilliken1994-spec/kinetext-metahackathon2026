## Findings (computed above)

1. **The job budget was not the direct killer.** None of the 12 TIMEOUT jobs reached the 420 s `maxJobWallTimeMs` (longest: 295.5 s); each died when ONE provider call passed the client's 120 s per-call timeout and the worker treated that `TIMEOUT` MetaError as fatal for the WHOLE lesson. Both the per-call timeout and a true budget expiry (`DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`) are covered by the fix.
2. **Spark Open calls are slow enough to hit the per-call timeout.** muse-spark-open had 11 timed-out calls across the evaluation vs 1 for muse-spark-1.3, and 30 calls at or above 100 s vs 4. Median latency: analyze 51.1 s (open) vs 30.9 s (1.3); component generate 40.8 s vs 29.3 s; component repair 24.2 s vs 25.1 s. P90 component generate 77.7 s (open) vs 52.8 s (1.3); the slowest open analyze call took 119.9 s — a hair under the 120 s cutoff. Repairs are NOT individually slower on open (p50 24.2 s vs 25.1 s); the timeouts sit in the generate/analyze tail.
3. **The open endpoint is slower per token, and the timeline component is the heaviest.** Median reasoning tokens per component generate are nearly equal (8049 open vs 7623 on 1.3) while the open median latency is 40.8 s vs 29.3 s: the shared Modal endpoint delivers High-effort reasoning more slowly. Analyze adds more thinking on top (10296 vs 8043 median reasoning tokens). 7 of the 12 terminal timed-out calls were timeline-v1 (the per-job tables show its generates/repairs carrying the largest reasoning volumes, e.g. 20k+ tokens), so the biggest component is the one most likely to cross 120 s.
4. **Analysis repairs are 4× more frequent on Spark Open and serialise ahead of every component.** muse-spark-open ran 86 analysis-repair calls (0.44 per job) vs 22 for muse-spark-1.3 (0.11 per job) — the summary.json cross-check agrees (86 vs 22). At median 13.3 s vs 9.2 s that is roughly 6 s vs 1 s of average pre-component time: a real contributor to the wall time, but seconds, not the main cause.
5. **Concurrency-2 queueing pushes the last part into the slow window.** Plan sizes are similar (1.93 parts/job open vs 1.95 on 1.3), but on a 3-part plan the third component only starts when one of the first two finishes: the TIMEOUT jobs with 3 parts show third starts +41 s and +44 s after the overview publish (per-job tables). Combined with the slow tail, the queued part or a second repair attempt is what crosses the 120 s per-call limit.
6. **Each timeout also wastes money.** A timed-out call settles at its reservation as `uncertain outcome`; the TIMEOUT jobs charged a total of 3.72 USD, of which 1.87 USD uncertain — and before the fix, all of it bought the teacher nothing.

## Fix (workers/generation/lesson.ts)

- A `TIMEOUT`/`DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` MetaError in a component generate or repair call is now COMPONENT-level: the charge is accounted at its own catch exactly as before (`errorCharge` + the one accumulator, `reservedUnknownUSD` kept), the component is marked failed with the fixed plain reason "Ran out of time before this part was finished." and the allow-listed detail (`TIMEOUT`/`DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`), and the job ends `succeeded` with a partial publish when at least the overview (and any passed parts) exists — the same rule as every other component-level failure.
- A repair call is never STARTED when the remaining job budget is below `MIN_REPAIR_BUDGET_MS` = 60 s; the component fails with the same timeout reason instead (no paid call). A timeout during the analysis stage (or an analysis repair that cannot start in time) still fails the whole job, as before.
- `NETWORK_ERROR` during a component call remains job-fatal (unchanged; see the report to the lead for the argument that the same partial-publish rule should apply to it).

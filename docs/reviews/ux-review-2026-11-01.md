# Independent visual and UI/UX review

**Reviewer:** GPT-6.1 Sol. **Date:** 2026-11-01. **Target:** running app on localhost:5173, stored data from localhost:8787. Teacher-preparation, classroom and three-minute hackathon-video lenses.

## Verdict

The strongest experience is already inside the product: the binary-search presentation puts a changing diagram beside a question, then reveals the next search range. DNA labels now land in the original figure's label boxes. Those are specific teaching tools, not a generic AI dashboard. Preserve them. [E35, E36, E32]

The default workspace still makes a teacher read before they can act. The laptop view is especially costly: the Drag activity starts at y=465, its sliders start at y=702, and its prediction starts at y=1291 in a 768-pixel viewport. The first screen sells an article summary more clearly than an interactive lesson. [M1, E12]

**A credibility issue remains in accessible historical results.** The requested pH lesson is consistent, but another stored pH lesson still displays “Water at pH six” at 14 and “Water at pH five” at 6.3, under “Checked against the page.” The validator fix does not make those stored results safe to teach. This is not evidence that the new validator failed; it is evidence that the historical presentation still carries the old assurance. [E26, E47, M3]

## Top 10 changes, ranked by impact per effort

S = a small local change; M = a component/layout pass; L = a broader flow change. These are estimates, not implementation promises. Credibility gates come before cosmetic return on effort.

### 1. Stop giving stale stored activities a current checked assurance

**Where:** historical pH run `job-20261031111156-12c77646`. [E47, M3]

**What:** it plots a six-label at 14 and a five-label at 6.3 while showing a checked badge.

**Why:** a teacher can reach a wrong diagram through the ordinary runs UI. A judge can see the same contradiction without reading a line of code.

**Direction:** keep original artifacts unchanged. Add a current-validation gate or a prominent historical-result warning. Withhold the affected activity from teaching mode until it passes current checks. Do not silently rewrite recorded evidence. The consistent requested pH lesson can remain the featured example. **Effort: M.**

### 2. Remove the answer from prediction case labels

**Where:** Electoral College presentation, before R. [E37, E39]

**What:** the selected 269-vote case is labelled “Candidate falls just short of the threshold.” Another chip says “Candidate earns a broad winning margin.” These tell the class the answer while the result is still concealed.

**Why:** this defeats the central predict-then-reveal teaching move more directly than a spacing problem does.

**Direction:** use neutral labels: “270 votes,” “269 votes,” and the actual third vote count. Show interpretation only after reveal. Apply the same answer-leak review to all case titles. **Effort: S.**

### 3. Call the IRS calculation a partial schedule, not total tax

**Where:** IRS data-table activity. [E28, E29]

**What:** the chart explicitly has a “No rate” segment up to $24,800, yet the result says “Total tax” and “Effective rate.” Its limitation is in a footnote.

**Why:** precise dollars and percentages imply a complete calculation. A teacher should not have to reconcile the claim with a missing-rate disclaimer.

**Direction:** put “Partial rate schedule” above the chart. Label the result “Tax from the listed bands” rather than “Total tax.” Put the omitted-rate warning beside the result, not below the teaching action. State which filing-status column is being used. Do not invent the missing band. **Effort: S for truthful framing; M if changing the calculator.**

### 4. Put a complete teaching action above the fold

**Where:** Drag, binary search, pH and Electoral College opening workspaces. [E10, E12, E15, E18, E24, M1]

**What:** overview, provenance and the first activity's introduction consume the opening. Binary search's question begins at y=1049 on desktop. The Electoral College threshold activity begins at y=1049. pH leads with a long quiz before its scale.

**Why:** a skimming judge sees reading and form controls before the distinctive interaction. A teacher needs the question and action together.

**Direction:** keep the title and one status line. Show a short learning challenge, then the strongest visual activity with its first question. Collapse the remaining overview into “Lesson notes.” Add compact activity navigation so pH can start at the ladder and civics at the vote threshold. Do not merely shrink the text. **Effort: M.**

### 5. Repair the creation panel's information architecture

**Where:** New lesson drawer. [E07–E09]

**What:** All 99, many subject chips, a long native selector and “Show all physics (13)” appear even while the water-cycle page is selected. The tour says 97 licensed pages. Paste your own text is a small disclosure below the selector.

**Why:** the teacher's first decision is obscured by library mechanics. The contradictory category link looks like unfinished state.

**Direction:** offer two clear entry modes: “Choose a page” and “Paste text.” Lead with three real stored examples that name the interaction produced. Keep the full library searchable by title and subject. Make the expansion link follow the selected category. Derive the public-library count separately from teacher/test sources. Keep unavailable model explanations in this read-only deployment. **Effort: M; category-link/count copy is S.**

### 6. Give premium simulations the compact workspace that the formula explorer already has

**Where:** lift simulation versus Drag formula explorer. [E14, E42, E48, E49]

**What:** Drag has sliders beside its graph. Lift still stacks the scene, equation, prediction, controls and readout. Scrolling to the controls cuts off the scene.

**Why:** the pupil should see the cause and consequence together. The same applies to a recording: scrolling away from the wing breaks the explanation.

**Direction:** scene and result on the left, first prediction and sliders on the right. Put additional cases behind a selector. Keep supporting explanations below. At narrower widths, use a compact teaching scene rather than shrinking the whole card. **Effort: M.**

### 7. Make repair details readable without exposing the checker transcript

**Where:** Electoral College repair drawer, technical details unchecked. [E21, E22]

**What:** JSON paths, `R-SUB-4`, “Checker said,” repeated policy findings and “Muse’s own mistake” remain visible. A useful before/after change is buried beneath them.

**Why:** the trust story is real, but reads like a debugging session. The label foregrounds model failure rather than the system's bounded response.

**Direction:** lead with “Automatic revision after a check failed.” Show one line each for issue, revision and outcome. Keep the real before/after diff. Put raw paths, check IDs, repeated findings and tokens behind the technical toggle. Distinguish “Teacher review pending” from “Automated checks passed” and “1 part withheld.” **Effort: M.**

### 8. Open presentation mode on a teaching hook, not a credit sheet

**Where:** binary-search presentation opening and toolbar. [E33, E34, E36]

**What:** the title slide gives substantial space to source, licence and disclaimer information. The later algorithm beat is much more compelling. The toolbar has small step labels relative to the slide.

**Why:** the opening seconds should establish a classroom question. The current opening is suitable for attribution, not demonstration.

**Direction:** open with the six bars and “Where does the search go next?” Keep required credit and AI disclosure readable but secondary. Offer “Start teaching” at the first activity, with overview and terms still available. Show a clearer current step and larger next/reveal affordances when the presenter moves the pointer. **Effort: M; changing the demo's starting beat is S.**

### 9. Make source evidence contextual by default

**Where:** source pane and linked overview. [E24, E47, E50]

**What:** a long source article remains visible regardless of the current activity. Many passages are underlined before a particular sentence is selected. The pH source includes repeated definition text.

**Why:** evidence is valuable, but currently competes with the lesson. The dense left column makes the app look closer to a reading/checking tool.

**Direction:** retain the full source mode for teacher preparation. Default to publisher, original link and the relevant passage. Expand to the full article on request. Use a faint passive mark and reserve strong gold emphasis for the active linked passage. Preserve bidirectional hover/focus linking. **Effort: M.**

### 10. Give prediction feedback one plain, useful voice

**Where:** lift feedback and generated lesson copy. [E49, E15, E33, E36]

**What:** a prediction of 18,000 N against 18,375 N gets “not close enough,” tolerance details and a second long feedback block. Repeated “Students learn that…” phrasing also carries into projector slides.

**Why:** accurate feedback can still feel punitive and machine-written. Repetition consumes room needed by the activity.

**Direction:** one comparison: “18,000 N predicted · 18,375 N calculated · just outside the 2% target.” Put numerical tolerance details in a disclosure. Use direct statements in generated summaries and questions. Preserve the original source quotation rather than rewriting it. **Effort: S for shared feedback copy; M for prompt/copy consistency across stored and new lessons.**

## What is good and must be preserved

- **The app-window identity.** Restrained chrome, a visible backdrop and the blue action colour work in both themes. No need for a new visual brand this close to recording. [E08, E50, E53]
- **Product-specific graphics.** Binary search's range changes, the lift wing, the pH interval and the original DNA figure give the product a reason to exist beyond summary generation. [E17, E26, E32, E48]
- **Predict before reveal.** Dashed question marks and stable answer locations create a usable classroom rhythm. Fix leaks, not the premise. [E34–E41, M2]
- **Evidence linking.** Hovering a Drag key point highlights both the statement and its source passage. This is a strong, quiet trust interaction. [E50]
- **Honest provenance and replay labels.** Recorded runs, AI-written parts, estimated costs and source credit are visible. Simplify their presentation without removing their truth. [E21, E43–E45]
- **The ready beat.** “Lesson ready” is a satisfying end to the build. It connects actual model activity to a working outcome. [E45]

### Specificity and anti-slop judgment

The activity graphics feel authored for teaching. The generic risk is not gradients or decorative cards; it is the repeated composition of summary, fine-print receipt and nested activity card. Repeated “Students learn…” sentences, “Muse’s own mistake,” numerous case chips and technical footnotes make the voice feel generated. A new colour palette would not solve that. Stronger task order and shorter copy would. The detector found no source-level flags, while these rendered UX issues remain. [E15, E22, E39, E49, M5]
